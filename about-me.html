About Me - The story of my life.

I grew up in Coalinga, CA until the age of 13, when my family moved to Lemoore, CA. I am 30 years old and I have 1 brother, who is older. Growing up, I had a lot of hobbies. I liked to play video games, both on the computer and on the PlayStation 1, 2, and eventually 3 by the time I was in high school. I also had a Honda CRF50 and I would ride with my brother across the street from my house out in the oil fields. Funnily enough, one of the things I spent a lot of time doing as a kid was what my friends and I called "Building Forts". In actuality, we liked to dig holes and put tarps and things like that up to provide us some shade. We would spend all day out there sometimes digging holes, drinking soda, and sometimes riding go-karts or doing things like airsoft/paintball battles.

When I moved to Lemoore, I spent a lot of time indoors, mostly playing games like Call of Duty or RuneScape. It was during this time where I really got my first taste in using someone else's code in order to do something. In Call of Duty, during the Modern Warfare 2 days, I was able to jailbreak my PS3 in order to override some save files in order to give a person whatever prestige they wanted. It's kind of funny to think of this looking back, because I know some people would use that capability to try and make money from people, but I feel like I have always felt like trying to sell to people was not for me. Instead, I typically offered to do this for good friends for free. (There was a lot of trust involved in the method I used, I would need to sign in to their PlayStation account in order to access certain files.) In hindsight, I feel like this kind of shows my character, though I also feel as though it's a little weird to point that out, but I am just typing as I think of it for now.

Moving on... On RuneScape, I would say this is where I gained a lot more interest in coding. If you are not familiar with RuneScape, there is quite the botting problem in that game. I would play RuneScape with my friend Eric. He also built forts with me a lot. Being that we were mischievous kids about 10 years old, we would always hear about all these cool methods for making money, leveling up skills, quests, and a ton of other things. One of the things we liked was making money in the game, and we would typically try and do things to manipulate people's bots in order to kill them for their loot. It really became an exercise in thinking about how these bots operate and ways to manipulate them. I remember the first time my friend lured a flax picking bot by wearing all green and letting the bot keep clicking on him as we led him to a bat, which killed him. We didn't even make a lot of money, but the feeling was exhilarating. I wanted to know about these bots and how people are using them. I remember thinking that we made 5,000 gold on one inventory or something like that, and how long it would take to get a million. I had no idea that there were people running hundreds or thousands of bots at a time making millions an hour. Eventually, I even used one of these bots to level my Magic skill to 99, which is the highest. This was when I was in high school, and I didn't really care for the game anymore anyways.

I remember at this point looking at one of the scripts that I had downloaded, which was essentially just an autoclicker that plugged into a greater script for anti-cheat purposes. It was at this point that I decided that I just wanted to play Call of Duty, coding was too hard, and that I'd rather not go to college.

So after high school, I ended up deciding to attend the community college near my house, Lemoore College, formerly West Hills College Lemoore. Initially, I went in thinking that it was time to learn more about computers and using them aside from games, YouTube, and fixing the internet at my house, so I went in and said I want to focus on my major as well as my general education classes, and I chose Computer Information Systems. I was still under the impression that coding was too hard for me, so I wanted to take that class to learn how to be more of a receptionist type of thing. In that coursework, I learned mostly things like Excel, Word, Outlook, and Access. We learned a bit about databases but honestly, none of that coursework really helped me when I went back for my IT degree and had to take a databases course, but I digress.

My intentions at Lemoore College were to get my Associate's Degree, transfer to Fresno State, and go from there. What ended up happening was that I learned that my CIS degree was not an Associate's Degree for Transfer. That meant if I wanted to transfer, I would need to pick an entirely different degree, and to me the one that had the most overlap with my interests, as well as with courses I've already taken, was Business Administration. I was able to finish up both Associate's Degrees in 2016, only taking 1 additional year. By that point, I was a bit disheartened with the idea of going to Fresno State, since part of me felt like I had been left behind on my final year by some of my friends I had met in college. I still ended up applying, but there were issues with the financial aid, so I decided I would rather just go and start finding a job.

I found a job pretty quickly working in the stockroom at DD's Discounts, and I worked there for almost 4 years. I had a lot of fun there, I even became the backup stock lead. I would be the acting stockroom lead when the stock lead would go on vacation, or if we worked more than 5 days a week, I would be acting lead on the additional days. Very simple responsibility if I'm being honest, just keeping track of how many units/hr of whatever department they were in while I also tried to keep up with productivity standards in my usual department, which was shoes. If you've ever purchased a pair of shoes from Hanford DD's Discounts from mid 2016 to beginning 2020, then you bought some shoes I touched. I'm also the reason you couldn't steal them. How does that feel? Probably not great. If you're reading this and you stole shoes during that time period, that was when I was training a new associate, definitely not my fault... I remember.

After DD's Discounts, I got a job as an Office Technician, where I would schedule appointments for the dental department, handle work orders, things like that. It was easy work, but it was basically what my CIS degree had taught me. At first, I did not want to let down my bosses, I wanted to show that I was a good worker and that I could be relied upon. I started in January of 2020, by February I could do that job blindfolded (metaphorically, I need to see my computer monitor silly =^] ).

I spent the next month doing my best to try and get the job down to the simplest set of things, with my own little time frames for completing things so that I could finish certain things, like going over inmate requests, at the same time as the dentist finishing an appointment. I would get the requests signed, input them into Dentrix, and put them on the schedule. I had pretty much everything down, and honestly I started to not feel challenged mentally.

As you may remember, March 2020 was when we in California first entered lockdown for the COVID-19 pandemic. I was 3 months in to this new job, and overnight everything changed. At the time, my girlfriend (now my wife) was living with us at my parents house, and she worked retail, so she was not working at all. My work was deemed essential, so I still had to report to work like everybody else, but all of a sudden we had constantly changing procedures and guidelines for everything. It was honestly kind of surreal.

Initially, the OTs in our positions were deemed necessary to show up every day, so I never even got to do telework initially. Eventually, as guidelines and procedures became more policies, a telework schedule was determined, where a person would work 1 week on, 1 week off. During the off time, we would be expected to complete trainings in the Learning Management System that the State uses. Eventually it turned to 1 week in office at the prison, and 2 weeks off. During this time, 1 OT and 1 Dentist would cover 3 yards instead of their typical 1 yard. This meant that instead of 6 dentists and 6 OTs at the prison, we were down to 2. We were only scheduling emergency procedures, and having to do a lot of walking between yards. It was honestly kind of fun at first, I had started to get bored before the lockdown, and eveything changing was fun for me.

After about a year of this weird schedule, I had picked up Call of Duty again, and honestly was just getting really bored with the work. It was at this time that I decided I wanted to try JavaScript. I did a little bit of practice learning some basics of JavaScript, like defining variables and stuff like that, but I ended up getting pretty sick, dealing with a lot of stomach pain and vomiting for a pretty long period of time. I also ended up having a child at this time and I got a job working nights as a stocker at Smart & Final overnight, since my wife and I don't really have very many good options for childcare. Eventually, I went back to school for my Information Technology Associate's degree from Lemoore College, and now I am kind of trying to expand my skills. I'm kind of at the same point where I'm not sure if I want to commit going to Fresno State for my Bachelor's, or just try to get some practical experience under my belt. I'm leaning towards just getting myself out there and hoping I can use my personal experiences working things like retail, sales, customer support and administration in order to be a great employee for someone who is looking for someone who is always trying to learn.

Well I will say, this wall of text doesn't really scream "PROFESSIONAL" to me, but I am a very honest person, and at a certain point if I want someone to hire me, I think they should really know the type of person that I am. I am a hard worker for sure. I take pride in my work, and I do not like to let others down. I like to help others, and at times I definitely a bit to a fault, since I would sometimes overestimate my ability to either assist in others' tasks, or I would underestimate how much was still needed on my own tasks. I still typically finish on time, it just gets stressful sometimes. I am always trying to learn. I want to learn how to do things in the best ways, and I also want to learn why things are the best. I still have a lot to learn when it comes to web development, and I also want to focus on perhaps creating a standalone application sometime as well. I am excited to learn, I just really need to point my direction in one way or another. Currently (June 4th, 2026) I am trying to expand my knowledge in C# and React.
